# Future Bending — two-minute retrodiction guide

Do not read this verbatim. Remember the four anchors: **broad passport → narrow record → behind schedule → institutional commitment**.

## Question 1 — What changed your mind?

“I entered the week thinking the best solution was a broad evidence passport for informal workers—something that could collect receipts and other activity and prove that a person could repay. The evidence and the build forced me to narrow that claim. Three supplier receipts can support recurring operating activity, but they cannot prove sales, profit, cash flow or repayment capacity. My final product therefore does not score or recommend. It shows three source-linked purchases, names every unassessed claim, and only becomes useful when an institution commits beforehand to a human review and a repair path. The persona test bent me again: the original green check made the user think credit was already approved, so I changed the hierarchy to say, first, that it is not an approval.”

## Question 2 — Where is 2041 actually landing?

“For this Mexican version, I think the future is technologically ahead of schedule but institutionally behind schedule. OCR, structured extraction, consent logs and source links already exist; that was not the binding constraint I saw. The binding constraint is credible evidence-to-action: an informal merchant can organize activity, but the record has no value unless a lender agrees in advance what it will unlock, gives a source-backed reason when it is insufficient, and funds a human reviewer who can reverse a result. The Golden Elephant’s risk is therefore arriving early—more data can already classify people—while meaningful contestability and institutional accountability are arriving late.”
