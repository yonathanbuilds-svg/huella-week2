# BLUEPRINT — Week 2 — Team 6

## Facts we agree on

- Informal economic activity can be real even when traditional financial systems cannot recognize it as acceptable evidence.
- Alternative data can expand access, but more scoring does not automatically mean fair inclusion.
- AI can extract and structure receipts, invoices, statements, and other records, but extraction is not the same as verification.
- We should not build another universal credit score.
- Users need control over what they share and must be able to inspect, correct, and challenge evidence.
- Association data such as family relationships, social graphs, contacts, and neighborhood proxies can create discriminatory outcomes and should be restricted.
- Evidence has little value unless a participating institution commits to doing something with it.
- Existing global systems already demonstrate permissioned data sharing, portability, provenance, and structured financial data; Mexico's opportunity is to adapt these ideas to informal economic evidence.

## Bets we chose

- Evidence is the better starting point than Explanation for informal Mexican borrowers. We will build around Evidence while testing whether lack of acceptable evidence is actually the primary bottleneck. **Dissent:** Money believes Explanation + Repair deserves greater weight.
- We will not build an AI that guesses why a proprietary lender rejected or priced someone. Explanation must come from source-backed evidence or verified lender reasons. **Dissent:** none.
- We will only build the evidence product with a participating institution that commits beforehand to a defined action when qualifying evidence is verified. **Dissent:** none.
- We will adapt and localize proven global infrastructure rather than pretend every component must be invented from zero. **Dissent:** none.

## Primary vacuum

**Evidence-to-Action for informal merchants:** turn user-controlled, source-linked informal business evidence into a record that a participating institution commits to act on.

## The conditions

1. Evidence must remain source-linked, and uncertainty must be disclosed; AI extraction cannot be presented as verification.
2. Sharing qualifying evidence must unlock a defined institutional action, such as advancement from automatic rejection to human review.
3. Every material evidence gap must be visible and accompanied by a realistic correction or repair path.
4. The system may not speculate about or reverse-engineer black-box lending decisions; explanations must be supported by evidence or verified institutional reasons.
5. **Shadow clause:** opportunity may not be determined by association. Family relationships, contact lists, social graphs, customer identities, precise home location, and neighborhood-level risk proxies may not be used to judge repayment ability.
6. Human appeal must have genuine authority to correct evidence and change an outcome when appropriate.

## Yonathan's declaration

**USER →** Build a Verified Operating Record for an informal merchant seeking inventory financing, showing verified activity, sources, missing evidence, and the institutional next step → **Condition 3.**

## Dissent preserved

- Money still believes that Explanation + Repair is more central than the team currently gives it credit for, especially because expanded access can still leave borrowers trapped at expensive rates without understanding how to improve their position.
- The team chose Evidence-to-Action as the primary vacuum, but Explanation + Repair survives as an essential layer inside the product rather than as a standalone system.
- The Adversary accepts the concept only if all six conditions are mandatory; weakening the Shadow Clause, user control, or meaningful appeal would reopen the objection that the product creates a new surveillance system.
